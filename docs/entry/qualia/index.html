Qualia in Consciousness

Mary knows everything about colour — in a black-and-white room.

2 MIN · EDITED
PHILOSOPHY OF MIND · THE RED ROOMWhat is it like to see red?Physics describes light. Nothing describes the redness.
IN ONE BREATH

Jackson's Mary knows all the physics of colour vision but has never seen red. When she leaves the room, does she learn something new? If yes, then facts about experience — — escape the physical story.

The tension

Brains are objects with mass and charge. Experiences are subjective: there is something it is like to taste coffee. The tension is the explanatory gap — why should any firing of neurons feel like anything at all?

The strongest objection

OBJECTION

The ability reply: Mary gains no new facts, only a new ability — to recognise, imagine, remember red. Knowledge-how, not knowledge-that. Jackson himself later accepted a version of this.

VS
REPLY

Abilities come apart from facts: Mary could already track every wavelength, yet seeing red teaches her what it is like — and that was exactly the disputed fact.
