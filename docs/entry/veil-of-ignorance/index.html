Rawls' Veil of Ignorance

Fair rules are the ones you'd pick before knowing your place.

3 MIN · EDITED
POLITICAL PHILOSOPHY · FAIRNESSDesign society without knowing who you'll be.Rich or poor, healthy or sick — you must bet blind.
IN ONE BREATH

Imagine choosing the laws of society without knowing your class, talent, sex, or beliefs. Rawls says whatever you'd accept from behind that veil — especially protections for the worst-off — is justice.

The tension

We want both liberty and equality, merit and need. Behind the veil, self-interest becomes fairness: you insure against being at the bottom because you might be. Critics ask whether real people could ever reason that impartially — or whether they should.

The strongest objection

OBJECTION

Harsanyi's objection: rational agents behind the veil would maximise average welfare, not protect the worst-off — they'd gamble. Rawls replies that justice isn't a gamble when your whole life is the stake.

VS
REPLY

The parties aren't gambling with chips but choosing a whole life with no exit. Maximin isn't timidity here — it is refusing to stake your life on odds.
