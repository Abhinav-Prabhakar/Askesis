Descartes' Cogito

I think, therefore I am — the one sentence that survives doubt.

3 MIN · EDITED
MODERN PHILOSOPHY · FIRST CERTAINTYDoubt everything. One thing survives: the doubter.The furnace of methodic doubt — and the one sentence that won't burn.
IN ONE BREATH

Descartes burns down every belief that can burn: senses deceive, dreams deceive, perhaps a demon deceives. One thing resists the fire — that doubting is happening. From that spark he rebuilds God, the world, and you.

The tension

Certainty seems impossible — every source of belief can mislead. Yet here is one thing that cannot be false while it is thought. The tension: the cogito is bedrock, but bedrock an inch wide. Everything Descartes builds on it must cross back over the very doubt it escaped.

'I' does not name an inner object the way 'table' names one. The grammar of the first person misleads metaphysics.

THE ARGUMENT, IN THREE LINES
P1I can doubt everything I seem to perceive — my hands, the sky, mathematics itself.
P2But doubting is thinking, and thinking must be happening for doubt to happen.
∴Therefore I exist — at least as a thinking thing — whenever I doubt.

The strongest objection

OBJECTION

Lichtenberg's thinness objection: the argument proves that thinking is going on, not a lasting I. The cogito gives a momentary subject — a spark, not a soul — and Descartes smuggles in far more than the spark contains.

VS
REPLY

Thin is enough: the cogito never promised a soul, only a starting point. An inch of bedrock still beats the swamp.
