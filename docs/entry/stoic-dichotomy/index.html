STOICISM ANCIENT 2 MIN READ

The Stoic Dichotomy

Control what you can. Release the rest.

2 MIN · EDITED
ANCIENT STOICISM · THE HANDBOOKSome things are up to us. Everything else isn't.Epictetus's sorting task — the whole school in one cut.
IN ONE BREATH

Epictetus opens the Handbook with a line: some things are up to us — opinion, impulse, desire — and everything else is not. The Dichotomy is a sorting task for a whole life: put each trouble in the right pile, then act only where your hands reach.

The tension

The cut looks clean until you test it: health, reputation, other people — all partly up to us. The trichotomy critics say the world is mostly half-measures. The tension: a philosophy of total sorting must survive a world of partial control.

01
EPICTETUS

Up to us: opinion, impulse, desire, aversion. Not up to us: body, property, reputation, office. Want only your own pile to change, and nothing can block you.

T
02
THE TRICHOTOMY

Most things are partly up to us — health, skill, friendship. Control is a dial, not a switch, and the Handbook's two piles need a middle shelf.

M
03
MODERN STOICS

Irvine, Holiday, the therapists: the Dichotomy works as practice — circle of control, premeditation, evening review. The use matters more than the metaphysics.

THE ARGUMENT, IN THREE LINES
P1Distress comes from demanding that externals obey our will.
P2Externals are not up to us; only our judgments are.
∴Therefore freedom from distress is sorting correctly — and willing only the pile that is yours.

The strongest objection

OBJECTION

The pile problem: grief, illness and injustice arrive uninvited and unassorted. Telling the bereaved 'that is not up to you' sounds less like wisdom than coldness — and sorting itself needs judgment, exactly what crisis impairs.

VS
REPLY

The Stoics never said don't grieve — they said grieve without the extra demand that the world obey. Sorting is triage, not anesthesia; even a shaky hand can sort one thing at a time.

“It's not things that upset us, but our judgments about them.”
Epictetus · Handbook, §5
