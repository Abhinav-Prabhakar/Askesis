On Free Will

Physics says causes. Morality says blame. Both can't be half-right.

3 MIN · EDITED
METAPHYSICS · THE BIG FORKCould you really have done otherwise?Three answers: yes, no, and — wrong question.
IN ONE BREATH

If every event has a cause, your next decision was fixed at the Big Bang. Compatibilists reply that freedom isn't escaping causes — it's acting from your own reasons. Libertarians insist the future is genuinely open; hard determinists say it only feels that way.

The tension

Praise and blame assume you could have done otherwise. Neuroscience and physics suggest a chain of causes with no gaps. The tension is practical: we can't live without holding each other responsible, and we can't think without taking causes seriously.

HARD DETERMINISTS

Every choice has sufficient prior causes. Freedom is an illusion — but punishment still works as a cause.

COMPATIBILISTS

Freedom is acting on your own desires without coercion, even in a determined world. Hume, Frankfurt, Dennett.

LIBERTARIANS

Some events — including choices — are genuinely undetermined. Agent-causation starts new chains.

THE ARGUMENT, IN THREE LINES
P1A free act is one you control — one where you, not a chip, a drug, or a gun, are the source.
P2Control needs your reasons to make the difference, not a break in physics.
∴Therefore responsibility survives even if is true. (The compatibilist case.)

The strongest objection

OBJECTION

The consequence argument: if the past and the laws fix everything, your reasons were fixed too — so 'your' control is borrowed. Compatibilists answer with Frankfurt cases, where no alternative exists yet the act is still yours.

VS
REPLY

Frankfurt cases: the chip stands ready but never fires, and the act is still yours. Control doesn't need open alternatives — it needs your reasons doing the work.
